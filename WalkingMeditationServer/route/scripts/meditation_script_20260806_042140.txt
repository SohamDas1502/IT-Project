[FOCUSED_ATTENTION]  
Welcome. Let us begin this walking meditation by bringing your full attention to the present moment, to your body, and the gentle movement of walking. As you stand or begin to walk, feel the connection your feet have with the ground beneath you. Notice the textures, the firmness, and the support given by the earth. (pause)

As you start to take slow, deliberate steps, bring your awareness to the muscles in your legs. Feel your calf muscles engage as you lift your foot, the gentle tightening and releasing with each step. Notice the subtle stretch in your thighs and the steady balance of your knees. Feel your hips gently rocking as your weight shifts from one leg to the other. (pause)

Right now, in Melbourne, the weather is partly sunny, with a soft light filtering through a sky that is about 64% covered by clouds. The temperature is a comfortable 15.1 degrees Celsius, with a gentle northwest wind flowing at about 13 kilometers per hour, sometimes gusting a bit more. Imagine the sensation of this mild, fresh air brushing softly against your skin as you walk. The air feels just right—neither too warm nor too cold—around 14.4 degrees, perfect for a calm and mindful walk. (pause)

Feel the rhythm of your breath matching the pace of your steps. With every inhale, feel energy filling your lungs. With every exhale, feel your body gently sinking deeper into relaxation and awareness. (pause)

Let your mind become fully absorbed in the sensation of walking. Notice the subtle movements in your feet as they lift, move forward, and gently touch down. Feel the pressure shift as you roll from heel to toe. Each step is a new experience, unique and vivid. (pause)

Be curious about the small details—the way your toes grip or release, the slight flex of your ankle, the small adjustments your body makes to keep you balanced. This focused attention is a way of tuning in to yourself, to the present moment, without rushing or pushing. (pause)

There is a freshness in the air—the dew point sits at about 5.9 degrees Celsius, which means the air feels cool but not cold or damp. This crispness can energize your senses. As you walk, you might feel the brush of this clean air on your face, or hear the faint rustle of leaves stirred by the gentle breeze from the northwest. (pause)

Your body is the anchor here. Notice any sensations—warmth, tightness, ease, or tension in your muscles. Observe them without judgment, simply noting what is present as you keep your focus on each step and breath. (pause)

Your attention is like a spotlight, shining gently on the experience of walking. If the mind wanders, gently bring it back to the feet, to the sensation of movement, to the feeling of being alive and in motion. (pause)

With each step, you cultivate a calm, steady focus. You are fully here. You are grounded in the present. The partly cloudy sky might shift above you, the clouds moving slowly in the light wind, reminding you of the peaceful flow of nature. (pause)

Allow this focused attention to deepen. Feel your muscles working smoothly, without strain. Your body knows how to walk, and your mind is simply observing this natural, effortless action. (pause)

Notice the subtle sounds around you—the quiet footsteps, the whisper of the wind, distant birds perhaps, the soft rustling of leaves. These sounds weave into your awareness, enhancing your sense of presence. (pause)

Your breathing remains calm and steady. Each inhale brings a fresh wave of energy, each exhale releases any tension. Your muscles respond with ease, your body balanced and relaxed. (pause)

As you continue walking, feel the softness in the soles of your feet. Notice the connection between your feet and the earth—it supports you, holds you firmly but gently. (pause)

This moment, this walking meditation, is a gift. A chance to slow down, to focus, to be fully present in your body and environment, united with the rhythm of your steps and the natural world around you. (pause)

Stay here for a few more steps, breathing in this calm awareness, breathing out any distractions or worries. Let your mind rest in this simple, clear focus. (pause)  

[COMPASSION MEDITATION]  
Now, as you find a comfortable place to pause and stand still, we will gently shift our attention to compassion—the warm, kind feeling we offer ourselves and others. (pause)

Begin by bringing to mind someone you care about deeply, someone who brings warmth to your heart. Imagine them standing before you, and silently wish for them to experience happiness and peace. (pause)

Feel the energy of this wish. Compassion is like a gentle light that flows from your heart, reaching out with kindness and understanding. You might feel a soft warmth in your chest as you think of this person’s well-being. (pause)

Now, extend this feeling of compassion to yourself. Sometimes, we are our own hardest critics, but here, we offer ourselves the same kindness we would offer a dear friend. (pause)

Say quietly in your mind, or simply feel the meaning: May I be safe. May I be happy. May I be healthy. May I live with ease. (pause)

If any difficult feelings arise—stress, frustration, or pain—acknowledge them gently without pushing them away. Compassion means accepting what is present, even when it is hard. (pause)

Imagine wrapping yourself in a soft, comforting embrace made from the warmth of your own compassion. Feel this embrace soothing and calming you from the inside out. (pause)

Now, widen this circle of compassion. Think of someone you find difficult or challenging. It might feel hard at first, but try to offer them a simple wish of well-being. Perhaps say quietly: May you be free from suffering. May you find peace. (pause)

Notice what happens inside you as you do this. You might feel resistance, or a softening, or even a surprising sense of connection. Compassion is not about fixing or judging, but about opening your heart to another human being. (pause)

Let this feeling grow stronger—like a gentle flame. Compassion does not wear out; it expands the more we practice it. It creates space for understanding and kindness in ourselves and the world around us. (pause)

Bring to mind a group of people—your community, your coworkers, or even all beings everywhere. Imagine your compassion flowing outwards in waves, reaching them with warmth and goodwill. (pause)

Say to yourself: May all beings be safe. May all beings be happy. May all beings be free from suffering. (pause)

If thoughts or feelings distract you, gently return your focus to these wishes, to the feeling of compassion flowing through you like a soft river. (pause)

Compassion helps us connect deeply with life. It reminds us we are not alone in our struggles or joys. It is a shared thread in the vast fabric of human experience. (pause)

Remember, compassion begins with small moments. A kind thought, a gentle word, a warm smile. It grows by simple acts of care and understanding, starting with yourself. (pause)

In this quiet moment, feel the strength and tenderness of your compassionate heart. Know that this heart can hold all feelings with kindness and courage. (pause)

Let your breath be soft and natural as you rest in this energy. Compassion is your true nature, waiting to be expressed in every step you take, every word you speak, every thought you hold. (pause)

Take a few more moments here, breathing in compassion and breathing out love to yourself and the world. (pause)  

[CLOSING MEDITATION]

Reflect now on the purpose of this practice—to prepare yourself for a stressful meeting ahead. Sometimes, before we face challenging situations, our minds race with worry, anticipation, or fear. This meditation is here to help you meet those moments with calm and clarity. (pause)

Remember the focus you cultivated—the awareness of each step, each breath. This same attentive presence can accompany you throughout your meeting, helping you stay grounded and clear. (pause)

Remember the compassion you gathered—the kindness you offered yourself and others. You can carry this compassionate heart into your meeting, softening any tension and opening space for understanding and patience. (pause)

When you feel overwhelmed or pressured, gently return to your breath. Feel it as an anchor amidst the noise. Let your breath remind you that you have the ability to pause, breathe, and respond with calm. (pause)

If your mind begins to spin with worries or what-ifs, acknowledge those thoughts with kindness, then let them pass like clouds moving through the sky. (pause)

Trust in the strength of your inner calm, built through this meditation. You are more capable than you realize. You are supported by your own steady presence. (pause)

As you prepare to move from this quiet space back into your day, set an intention for how you want to be in your meeting. Maybe it is to listen openly, to speak clearly, to remain patient, or simply to be present. (pause)

Know that stress does not have to overwhelm you. With mindful breathing and compassionate awareness, you can meet it with grace. (pause)
